# Period drill and fighting motion

Sources: "Manual Exercise as Ordered by His Majesty in the Year 1764"; Bland, "A Treatise of Military Discipline" (1727); the French "Ordonnance du Roi pour régler l'exercice de l'infanterie" (1754); the Spanish "Ordenanzas de S. M. para el régimen, disciplina, subordinación y servicio de sus ejércitos" (1768); Angelo, "L'École des armes" (1763); Roworth, "The Art of Defence on Foot with the Broad-Sword and Sabre" (1798). Drill books differ in detail and over the years: use the structure and the key poses, not exact word-for-word choreography. Frame counts come from the game (see `rigging-and-animation.md`).

## British 1764 Manual Exercise: the firelock

A command is cautionary, then executive; each motion is a count of 1-3 movements, brisk between fixed positions, held a beat. The musket (Land Pattern, about 5 kg, about 1.5 m, bayonet adds about 0.45 m) rests on the right side at **Shoulder** (butt in the right hand under the cock, barrel against the shoulder).

| motion | key body positions | time (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **Poise** | firelock off the shoulder in front, lock outward, left hand under the barrel at chest height, right forearm horizontal | 1 s |
| **Cock** | right thumb on the cock, pulled to full cock; the firelock stays near the chest, elbow down | 0.5 s |
| **Present** | butt snapped to the right shoulder, cheek on the stock, left arm extended under the barrel; body slightly forward, the weight on the left (front) leg, right foot drawn back a few inches | 1 s |
| **Fire** | the right forefinger pulls the trigger; the piece recoils into the shoulder and is held; muzzle climbs a few degrees | 0.2 s + 0.5 s hold |
| **Half-cock** | the piece comes off the shoulder to the left hand's chest position, thumb lowers the cock to half-cock; the pan is open | 1 s |
| **Handle cartridge** | the right hand reaches to the cartridge box on the right hip, takes a cartridge, brings it to the mouth | 1.5 s |
| **Prime** | tear the cartridge with the teeth; the powder in the pan; the right hand returns to the piece | 1.5 s |
| **Shut pan** | the frizzen is closed with the right hand; the firelock held upright in front | 0.5 s |
| **Cast about** | the firelock is turned and carried to the left, muzzle up, butt at the hip (approximately; read the manual for the foot movement), ready to load | 1 s |
| **Load** | the rest of the powder poured down the barrel, the ball and paper dropped in | 1.5 s |
| **Draw ramrods** | the right hand draws the ramrod, flips it to the muzzle | 1 s |
| **Ram down** | the rod is pushed down twice to seat the charge | 1.5-2 s |
| **Return ramrods** | the rod returns to the pipes | 1 s |
| **Shoulder** | back to the right shoulder, in two movements | 1 s |

Total reload: about 12-20 s by the book. Soldiers in the field managed about 3-4 shots a minute; faster drills were fast-fire (loading without the rest). **Platoon firing**: the order is the "make ready" (cock), "present", "fire" at a signal; the line moves together; the motions of everyone are in sync by the count.

### French and Spanish counterparts (outline)

- **French 1754:** "Portez vos armes", "Apprêtez vos armes" (cock), "En joue" (present), "Feu", "Haut les armes" (recover), then loading: open the pan, take the cartridge, tear it, prime, shut the pan, "Passez l'arme" to the left (cast about), charge, draw rammers, ram down, return rammers, shoulder.
- **Spanish 1768:** the line formed by files with "Preparen", "Apunten", "Fuego", then "Cargar"; the sequence is the same and the loading is in time (about 3-4 rounds a minute). Spaniards fired in two or three ranks; the first rank kneeling when three-deep.
- All three: the **musket held with the butt to the right shoulder; weight forward; head turned straight along the stock**. Differences are the foot positions and the hand on the cock; at 45 px the three are visually the same: tell them apart by uniform and gear, not by the choreography.

## Aimed fire

| element | rule |
|---|---|
| head | the cheek rests on the side of the butt (the Brown Bess has no raised comb), the head inclined along the stock, the aiming eye near the lock side; the other eye closed or open |
| stock | butt pressed into the pocket of the right shoulder, the toe a little inside the joint; the muzzle slightly above horizontal for 60-80 m |
| elbows | right elbow raised to shoulder height or just below, pointing out and slightly back; left elbow under the barrel, forward and down; the forearms forming a near-right angle |
| feet and weight | feet about shoulder-width, the left foot toward the target, weight on the leading (left) foot, the knee slightly bent; right foot back, heel raised; torso turned about 30-45 deg right shoulder back |
| second rank | closes up with half a pace to the right so that each man stands behind a gap between the front-rank men; the muzzle goes past the front man's right shoulder, above his ear and about 10 cm away from it; the barrel is angled a few degrees up |
| after firing | the butt lowered to the hip without delay; the rear-rank man steps back to reload |

## Bayonet

- **Charge bayonets:** the left foot forward, the left knee bent, the right leg straight behind; the piece level at hip or waist height, the butt under or by the right hip, the left hand extended under the barrel; bayonet at chest to face height of the opponent.
- **Thrust:** a straight push with the left arm and the lunge of the left leg (rear heel stays down), the point travelling in a line, then a quick recovery to the guard. The period British drill is simple (push, with some guards and parries). In the line the bayonet charge moves as a block with a close step.
- At 45 px: a long line sloping forward, bent front knee; it reads as a spiky lance. Keep the musket plus bayonet at about 1.9-2 m.

## Pike, halberd, spontoon

| weapon | length | handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Pike** (16th-17th C. pikemen, Spanish tercios) | 4-5.5 m | at "order" butt on the ground, the left hand about shoulder height; "advance" at the right shoulder or couched under the right arm with the point at chest to face height; the rear hand low at the hip, the front hand a third up the shaft, the shaft flexing; the body braced, feet apart; the tip bobs little |
| **Halberd** (sergeants) | 2.1-2.4 m | the axe-head and spike; carried at the shoulder or in the right hand; thrust with the spike, chop with the axe, pull with the hook; used also to dress the ranks |
| **Spontoon** (officers, NCOs, 18th C.) | about 1.8-2.1 m | a short pike with a blade; thrust or carried by the right hand, point up; officer's salute with the weapon |

## Sword

- **Smallsword (officers, Angelo 1763):** thrusts only, lunge with the rear leg straight; guards in the numbered lines (prime, seconde, tierce, quarte, quinte); the sword arm extended in a line with the shoulder, the point at the opponent's face and chest; the free arm raised behind and bent for balance.
- **Broadsword, cutlass, hanger, sabre (infantry, cavalry, Highland, naval):** the cuts come from the shoulder or elbow in numbered diagonals: down from the right shoulder to the left, down from the left to the right, a horizontal cut at the waist or neck, a rising cut from the lower side, a vertical cut to the head, and the point (thrust). Guards (Roworth): a guard that covers the head with the sword arm raised and the blade angled (the "St George's" or hanging guard), an inside and an outside guard at chest level, and a low guard with the point toward the ground. Cavalry cut from the saddle with the wrist and elbow, the arm swing in an arc, return to the guard.
- A cut starts with an anticipation (the hand back and up) and ends in follow-through with the blade at the opposite hip; the body turns with the cut; the weight shifts to the front foot on the extension.

## Drummer, fifer, colour bearer

- **Drummer:** the drum hangs from a shoulder belt on the left hip, tilted so the top slants away (the head angled about 20-30 deg), the sticks held in each hand near the end, the forearms raised to a horizontal at elbow height 10-20 cm from the body; the head up; the stroke is a wrist flick of 10-15 cm; marching steps in time.
- **Fifer:** the fife held across the lips, the head turned slightly to the right, the elbows lifted out at about 70-90 deg, the fingers spread over the holes; a small bob in step; no equipment in the hands.
- **Colour bearer (ensign):** the pole about 2.4-2.7 m with the flag about 1.8 m square (approximately); carried in both hands or the right hand with the butt in a belt socket at the hip, the colours upright or tilted forward over the body; he stands in the centre of the line, behind or amid the sergeants; on the march the flag streams; lowered to salute; with the colour guard in step.

## Native fighting

| action | essentials |
|---|---|
| **Tomahawk throw** | overhand from beside the head; the rear foot steps and the front arm points; the hawk is released near the ear at the end of the arm's arc; the hand follows to the target; one to one-and-a-half rotations at 5-8 m; the body leans forward at release |
| **Tomahawk strike** | held 1/3 up the haft (40-50 cm total); overhand or diagonal hacks from the shoulder with a snap at the wrist; the other hand grabs or shields; the follow-through goes across the body; short, fast strokes |
| **Bow draw** | the left (bow) arm extended, the right drawing arm brought to the face or chest with the elbow in line with the arrow, the shoulders in line; the bows of the Eastern Woodlands are longer (about 1.2-1.5 m) and drawn to the chest or cheek; horse peoples use short bows (about 0.9-1.1 m); the release returns the bow hand forward and the string hand backward; a rapid shot cycle of 2-3 s |
| **War club** | the ball-headed or gunstock club in one hand, wide round swings from the shoulder with the hips turning, weight on the lead foot; recovery to the guard with the club head near the shoulder; a low swing to the knees |
| **General** | a crouched, light-footed stance, hips low, the weight on the balls of the feet; quick darts instead of a block line; shields (hide, in the plains) held in the off hand |

## What reads at 45 px versus up close

- At 45 px (1 px about 4 cm) only whole-body silhouette matters: the musket line and angle (shoulder, present, fire, load), the leaning posture, the arms' relative height, and the periodic motion of the arm in reload. Changes smaller than about 10 deg or 10 cm vanish: exaggerate key poses by 20-30 % and keep distinct hold times.
- Reads: bayonet charge, the raised pike, a swung sword (arc), the bow draw (the line), the drum angle, the colours.
- Only up close: the thumb on the cock, the cheek weld, the foot angles, the cartridge to the mouth, the ramrod stroke, the fingers, the face.
- Check key poses against the sprite frames, and keep the cadence, since timing is what a drill is.
