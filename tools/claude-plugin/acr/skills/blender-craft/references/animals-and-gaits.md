# Animals and gaits

Sources: Muybridge, "Animals in Motion" (1899) and "The Horse in Motion" (1878); Hildebrand's gait studies of quadrupeds (Science, 1965 and later); Hildebrand and Alexander, "Principles of Animal Locomotion"; Goubaux and Barrier, "The Exterior of the Horse"; Williams, "The Animator's Survival Kit" (quadruped chapter). Numbers are approximate; use them to set ranges, then match the game's frame counts.

## Military horse, about 1760

Cavalry and dragoon horses ran about 14.2-15.2 hands (1 hand = 10.16 cm: 14.2 hh = 1.47 m, 15.2 hh = 1.58 m at the withers); heavier heavy-cavalry horses to about 16 hh; Spanish/colonial horses and Plains ponies 13.2-14.2 hh (1.38-1.47 m).

| feature | rule |
|---|---|
| body | roughly square: withers height about equals length from point of shoulder to point of buttock |
| chest depth | about half of withers height; leg length (elbow to ground) about equals chest depth |
| head | length about 0.4 of withers height; neck about 1.2-1.5 head lengths on top |
| shoulder / pastern | blade slopes about 45-50 deg; pasterns about 45-55 deg |
| back / loin | short (about 3 handspans), croup slopes about 20-25 deg |
| hocks / knees | hock angle about 150 deg standing; the knee (carpus) is a flat hinge, no backwards bend |
| cavalry type | plain, deep-chested, strong bone; cannon bone about 0.2 of withers height; thick neck and heavy hindquarters in draft crosses |
| mass | 400-500 kg (light) to 550+ kg; rider and kit 90-110 kg |

Muscle and bone landmarks that must exist: withers, point of shoulder, elbow behind the girth, knee and fetlock, hock, stifle on the flank, and the dorsal line. A horse is not a deer: no long thin legs.

## Footfalls and timing

Hoof order given for a stride that starts on the left hind; L/R = left/right, H/F = hind/fore. Duty factor = fraction of the stride a hoof is on the ground. The stride lasts one full cycle of one leg.

| gait | beats | order of hoofs | duty factor | stride time | speed | suspension |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk | 4, even (25 % apart) | LH, LF, RH, RF | 0.65-0.70 (2-3 hooves down) | about 1.0-1.2 s | 1.4-1.8 m/s | none |
| trot | 2 (diagonal pairs) | LH+RF, then RH+LF | 0.40-0.55 (drops with speed) | about 0.6-0.8 s | 3-4.5 m/s | 2 per stride, after each pair |
| canter (right lead) | 3 | LH, then RH+LF together, then RF (leading); then suspension | about 0.3-0.45 | about 0.5-0.7 s | 5-7 m/s | 1, after the leading foreleg |
| gallop (right lead) | 4, uneven | LH, RH, LF, RF; then suspension | about 0.2-0.3 | about 0.4-0.5 s | 8-12 m/s (military 7-9) | 1, bigger; in the gathered phase the legs are bunched under the belly, in the extended phase stretched out |

Notes: Muybridge's plates (1878) first showed the gathered suspension of the gallop (all four feet off the ground under the body, not outstretched, as the old paintings had it). The stride lengths are about 1.6-1.9 m (walk), 2.5-3.5 m (trot), 3-4 m (canter), 5-7 m (gallop) for a 15-hand horse. A lead is the leg that lands last; the horse leans slightly toward the lead's side on curves.

## Head, neck and body motion

- **Walk:** the head nods once per stride (about 1 Hz): down as a foreleg lands, up as it lifts; the neck lengthens and shortens by 5-8 cm. The back sways side to side (about 2 deg) and the hip rises on the side of the leg that is swinging.
- **Trot:** head still, neck level; the back stays rigid; the body rises and falls twice per stride (about 4-6 cm).
- **Canter:** the head nods once per stride, down on the diagonal pair and up through the leading leg and suspension; the spine rocks nose-up and nose-down like a rocking horse.
- **Gallop:** the neck extends fully forward and low; the head moves up and down once per stride with the spine flexing and extending (the back arches in the gathered phase, hollows in the extended); the tail streams.
- Overlap: the neck leads the body by about 10-15 % of a stride; the tail lags by about 20 %; ears follow the head's pitch. Heavy gaits need the root to dip when the leading foreleg lands.

## Rider coupling

| gait | rider |
|---|---|
| walk | pelvis moves in a small figure of eight, shifting side to side with the horse's hind legs; hands follow the head nod (about 2-3 cm); upper body stays quiet |
| trot | sitting: pelvis bounces vertically 3-5 cm, upper body absorbs it, sabre and saddle gear bounce; rising: stands on every other beat (rise on the outside diagonal), 10-15 cm up/down; trotting troops usually sit when tight |
| canter | pelvis rocks forward and back along the horse's spine once per stride (about 5 deg); a slight forward lean of 5-10 deg; hands move forward and back with the head |
| gallop | 15-25 deg forward lean, weight on stirrups with open hip, seat just clear of the saddle; hands forward along the neck; coat skirts and plume stream; sword arm held steady |

Seat of the period's cavalry: upright, long stirrup, straight leg (calf behind the girth), heels down; Plains riders ride with a shorter leg, bareback or a pad, and lean with the horse. Feet follow the horse (about 5-8 cm swing); the head stays steady relative to the horse's motion; the rider's centre of mass moves less than the horse's.

## Other animals

| animal | build | gait essentials |
|---|---|---|
| **Black bear** (also brown bear) | shoulder 0.8-1.0 m (grizzly 1.0-1.3 m), body 1.5-2 m, 90-200 kg (grizzly up to 400 kg); plantigrade, heavy shoulders, head low, short tail, small ears | walk is a lateral sequence with the pigeon-toed paws, hips and shoulders rolling; gallop is a bound (hind legs land ahead of the front); sits and stands on hind legs to look (head at 1.5-2 m); rears with a balance shift back |
| **Bison** | shoulder (hump) 1.5-1.9 m, length 2.1-3.5 m, 450-900 kg; huge head carried low, short thick neck, massive hump over the shoulder, thin hindquarters, beard and shag | a slow, rolling walk; a trot; the gallop at about 15 m/s with head low, tail up when alarmed; the head swings in line with the body; avoid horse-like fluid motion |
| **Elk (wapiti)** | bull shoulder 1.3-1.5 m, 250-450 kg, antlers span up to 1.2 m, long legs, long neck | a walk similar to a horse; a long trot (the main travel gait); a bound or gallop when fleeing; the bull's head carried back to keep the antlers off the shoulders; the neck swells in rut |
| **American alligator** | 2.5-4 m adults (about 4.5 m rare), height off the ground 0.2-0.4 m; the tail is about half the length; the sprawled legs; broad head | belly-crawl or high walk (body lifted, diagonal footfall like a slow walk); the tail sweeps side to side (lateral undulation); on land fast for a few metres (up to about 10-15 km/h); in water the legs tuck back and the tail drives; the jaws snap sideways with a head whip |
| **Jaguar** | body 1.1-1.85 m, tail 0.45-0.75 m, shoulder 0.65-0.75 m, 55-95 kg; stocky, short legs, broad head, powerful forelimbs | stalk: belly low, shoulder blades rising above the back line, a lateral-sequence walk; trot is fluid; the gallop is a rotary gallop with a strongly flexing spine, hind feet landing in front of the fore feet at extension; climbs and swims; the pounce coils back, then springs |

Rules: weight shows in slow ease-in and ease-out; legs of small, heavy animals swing less and stay on the ground longer (larger duty factor). Match stride length to speed: slip is a sliding foot, the most visible error.

## Death and falls of large animals

- **Timing:** the top of a 1.5 m horse needs at least about 0.55 s to fall freely (t = sqrt(2h/g)); a real collapse takes about 1-1.5 s because the legs buckle. Faster reads as a rag doll, slower as a ballet.
- **Shot horse at speed:** the front legs fold at the knees first, the nose digs in, the neck drops, the forequarters roll, the hindquarters are carried over (an end-over-end tumble), then a slide of 3-6 m. A rider is thrown forward and over the head. At a standstill the horse sinks onto the knees, then the hindquarters, then rolls onto a side (usually the side away from the wound) with the legs stiff, the neck and head extended and the body rolling slightly back, then the legs paddle for 1-2 s and the breath fades.
- **Bison and elk:** the forelegs buckle, the hump or the antlers dig into the ground; they roll onto a side; the head stays up for a moment.
- **Bear:** the hindquarters or the front give; the animal slumps on the chest, then rolls; the paws twitch.
- **Alligator and reptiles:** stay flat; the tail thrashes for a few seconds, the legs relax outward; may roll belly-up.
- **Jaguar:** the spring dies mid-air and drops in a limp heap; the spine flexes with the limbs flung.
- Always: the centre of mass drops first, the limbs trail by 2-4 frames, then secondary motion (ears, tail, head) settles; the final frame equals the lying pose (see `rigging-and-animation.md`); ground contact must not slide after the animal is down.
